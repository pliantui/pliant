Pliant is an open-source project for building apps that their users can reshape. This page covers its license, how to take part, and the work it builds on.

## License

Pliant is free and open source under the [MIT License](https://github.com/pliantui/pliant/blob/main/LICENSE.md). You can use it in commercial and closed-source apps.

The components you copy into your project are yours to change, like any shadcn component.

## Get involved

- **Questions and ideas:** open an [issue on GitHub](https://github.com/pliantui/pliant/issues).
- **Contributing:** read [CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/pliantui/pliant/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) for how to set up the repository and open a pull request.
- **Security:** report vulnerabilities privately, as [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/pliantui/pliant/blob/main/SECURITY.md) describes. Please don't open a public issue.
- **What's planned:** see the [roadmap](/docs/roadmap).

## Ideas it builds on

Pliant's core idea, that people should be able to reshape the software they use, comes from a long line of work. We're grateful to:

- **Alan Kay** and the Smalltalk team at Xerox PARC, for the idea of the computer as a medium its users can change.
- **Bill Atkinson** and **HyperCard**, for showing that people who don't think of themselves as programmers will happily build their own tools.
- **Dan Bricklin** and **Bob Frankston**, whose VisiCalc started the spreadsheet, still the most widely used way people shape software to their own work.
- **Bret Victor** and **Dynamicland**, for imagining computing that a whole room can see and change.
- **Ink & Switch**, whose essay [Malleable software](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/malleable-software/) gave this space its clearest map.

The way Pliant's docs are written follows [Diátaxis](https://diataxis.fr), the [Google developer documentation style guide](https://developers.google.com/style) and the [Write the Docs](https://www.writethedocs.org) community.

See [Why Pliant](/docs/why) for how these ideas shape Pliant.
