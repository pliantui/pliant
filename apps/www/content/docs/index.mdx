Pliant lets the people who use your shadcn app change how its pages are laid out for themselves. On an orders page, one person can put the overdue count first and hide the revenue card, while a colleague sorts the table by value and drops the notes column. Each person's version is saved for them, and your code doesn't change.

The page looks the same as it did before you added Pliant. `PliantRoot` loads and saves each user's changes for the page, and `PliantRegion` renders the cards the way the user arranged them. Cards have no controls of their own: with the [ask panel](/docs/ask-panel) added, a user can ask to move the **Overdue** card to the front, hide **Revenue** or show the row as a two-column grid, and the page stays that way the next time they open it. Try it in the [orders example](/examples/orders).

  Pliant is experimental. Its APIs can change in any release.
</Callout>

## What it adds to your app

Most apps grow these features one at a time: a sort that's remembered per user, a column picker, a layout reset. With Pliant, each of them is a change saved for the user in the same way, so you don't build and store them separately.

- Tables remember each user's sort and visible columns. A **Reset** button in the toolbar brings back your defaults.
- Through the ask panel, groups of cards and sections can be reordered, hidden and laid out as a row or a grid.
- Team or organization defaults can reorder a form's fields and hide optional ones. Required fields always stay.
- With the optional ask panel, a user can point at the table and type "only overdue orders". They see the result as a preview and choose whether to keep it.

[What users can do](/docs/what-users-can-do) lists every change.

## What Pliant doesn't do

Pliant doesn't replace your components. You keep your `components/ui`, and Pliant's own components, which you copy in with the shadcn CLI, render your button, table and menu.

Users can't add content of their own or change what a page does. They can rearrange, hide, sort and filter what you built, and nothing else.

Hiding a column doesn't protect it. The data still reaches the browser, so access control stays on your server, where it is today.

AI is optional. When you use it, the AI can only make the same kinds of changes users can make by hand, and every change is checked before it applies. It never writes code and never sees your rows. Pliant sends no analytics of its own.
