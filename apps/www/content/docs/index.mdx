Pliant lets the people who use your app reshape it to fit their work. They sort and hide columns, rearrange cards and ask AI for a change in plain words. Each person's changes are saved and come back on every visit, and your code stays exactly as you wrote it.

`PliantRoot` keeps the changes for one page. `PliantRegion` renders your cards as they are, until a user changes them. With no changes, the page renders exactly as you wrote it.

## Why it exists

Every app with tables and dashboards ends up needing the same thing. Users want the columns they care about, sorted the way they work, with the cards they check first at the top. Teams want a shared default. And all of it has to survive a reload.

Most teams build this by hand, one feature at a time: a sort that's saved per user, a column picker, a "reset layout" button.

Pliant is that layer, built once.

You keep your app and your shadcn components. Pliant stores what each user changed and applies it on top, along with any team or organization defaults you give it.

## What your users get

- **Tables that remember.** Their sort and the columns they show come back on every visit.
- **Pages they can arrange.** Cards and sections can be reordered, hidden, resized and laid out in a grid.
- **Changes in plain words.** With the optional ask panel, a user can point at part of the page and type "put overdue orders first". They see a preview, and nothing is saved until they choose to keep it.
- **A way back.** One Reset button drops their changes to a table and brings back your defaults.

See [What users can do](/docs/what-users-can-do) for every capability.

## What Pliant is not

**Not a component library.** You keep your own `components/ui`. Pliant adds a few thin components around the parts of the page users can change, and they render your own button, table and menu.

**Not a page builder.** Users can't add arbitrary content or write code. They rearrange, hide, resize, sort and filter what you built.

**Not a security boundary.** Hiding a column is a display preference, and the data still reaches the browser. Keep access control on your server, as you do today.

## Principles

### Works without AI

Sorting a table, choosing its columns and arranging a page all work by hand, with no AI involved. AI is one more way to make the same changes, and you can leave it out.

### Uses your design system

Pliant's components are copied into your project with the shadcn CLI, like any shadcn component. They use your theme and your `components/ui`, so the page still looks like your app.

### Safe by construction

AI never writes code for your app. A change is a small JSON document built from a fixed list of operations, such as "sort by value" or "hide the notes column". Pliant checks every change before it applies, and skips anything that isn't on the list.

### Removable

Pliant only adds a layer on top. Clear the changes and the page renders as you wrote it. Remove Pliant and you swap its components for the plain elements underneath. Your components and your data are untouched.

### No telemetry

Pliant sends no analytics. In the browser, it talks only to the storage you choose and, if you add the ask panel, your own server route.
