Pliant lets the people who use your shadcn app reshape its pages for themselves. A user points at part of a page, such as the orders table, and asks for what they need, such as only the overdue orders, or the overdue card first. The page changes in place, and once they keep the change it's saved for them. Your code doesn't change, and a colleague on the same page still sees their own version.

The difference is that a user can now drag the **Overdue** card to the front, hide **Revenue** with **Adjust by hand** in its **Customize** menu, or switch the row to a two-column grid, and see it that way the next time they open the page. `PliantRoot` loads and saves those changes for the page, and `PliantRegion` renders the cards the way the user arranged them. Try it in the [orders example](/examples/orders).

## Asking for a change

With the optional [ask panel](/docs/ai), users don't hunt for a setting. They right-click the part of the page they mean, or use its **Customize** button, choose **Ask for a change…** and type what they want. Every Pliant action lives in that one [Customize menu](/docs/concepts/customize-menu), so Pliant adds no buttons of its own to your page. The change shows right there as a preview, and nothing is saved until they choose **Lock in**.

The asks worth having are the ones you'd never build a control for, because each user wants something different. One person adds a column with each order's share of total value. Another turns overdue orders red, groups the table by region with a total for each, or adds a chart of value by region under the metric cards. A support agent makes the ticket form start at their usual priority. [What users can do](/docs/what-users-can-do) lists every change.

## Built on your shadcn components

Pliant's components, such as `pliant-region` and `pliant-data-table`, are copied into your project with the shadcn CLI, so their source is yours to read and edit like any shadcn component. They render your own `components/ui` button, table and menu, which means a reshaped page still looks like the rest of your app.

Per-user preferences usually arrive one feature at a time, each with its own storage, starting with a remembered sort. With Pliant, every one of them is a change saved for the user in the same way.

## How changes stay safe

The AI never writes code and never sees your rows. When a user asks, the model gets a description of the part they pointed at, such as its columns, their labels and what may change. It answers with a list of [operations](/docs/concepts/operations) from a fixed catalog, such as "filter to overdue" or "move this card first". Pliant checks every operation against the catalog and the component's rules on your server, and again in the browser, before anything shows. Changes users make by hand use the same catalog, and the browser checks each operation before it applies. [What the AI sees and never sees](/docs/ai/what-the-ai-sees) has the details.

AI is optional, and Pliant sends no analytics of its own.

## What Pliant doesn't do

Users can't change your data or what a page does. Every change is to how they see what you built: its order, layout, columns, highlights and views of data the page already shows.

Required form fields always stay. Hiding a column doesn't protect it, because the data still reaches the browser, so access control stays on your server, where it is today.
