Every operation is checked against these rules as it applies, whether it came from a user, the ask panel or a team default. [Guardrails](/docs/concepts/guardrails) explains why they exist; this page gives the exact behaviour.

  defaults must pass the field's schema, and fields stay in their form. Any
  other slot or column a user can see can be hidden or moved, and semantic prop

  these options to `resolveLayers` and `validateOverlay` from `@pliantui/core`.
  Render a notice users must always read outside Pliant's regions.
</Callout>

## How a rule skips an operation

Operations apply one at a time, from the lowest layer up. Each one that would break a rule, given the operations before it, is skipped and reported with the rule's name. When layers combine to break a rule, the operation from the highest layer involved is the one skipped. A lower layer's operation is judged only by the operations before it, so it's skipped even if a higher layer would undo it.

`validateOverlay` rejects an overlay that contains such an operation.

| Rule             | What it keeps                                                           |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `stays-visible`  | Parts flagged `critical`, and form fields flagged `required`, on screen |
| `stays-clear`    | A `destructive` action away from the `primary` action                   |
| `free-shortcut`  | Shortcuts off reserved keys and keys another action has                 |
| `keeps-meaning`  | Semantic prop values, such as a `destructive` variant                   |
| `valid-default`  | Form field defaults valid against the field's schema                    |
| `stays-in-place` | Form fields inside their own form                                       |

## Critical and required parts stay visible

A node or form field flagged `critical` or `required` can't be hidden, collapsed, demoted or removed, directly or through a node that holds it. It can't be moved into a hidden node, or into a cycle of nodes.

## Destructive actions stay clear of the primary action

An action flagged `destructive` never ends up closer to the action flagged `primary` in its group than your page puts it. A group is either a node's slots or one of a node's item collections, such as the `actions` of `orders-toolbar`. For slots, the group is the node that holds the primary action, and actions in wrappers inside it count as part of it. A destructive action in another group isn't judged against this primary action, so moving it to another node is allowed.

From far to close, a destructive action is apart from the primary action, right after it, or above it:

- In the order the group renders, a destructive action is above the primary action when it comes first, and right after it when no shown action sits between them. Moves, `pin start`, `pin end` and wraps all count, and a wrapper's actions count in its place. Hidden actions keep their place but don't keep the two apart, and neither does a wrapper on its own.
- A demoted or collapsed action, or one in a collapsed wrapper, has left the group's bar for its overflow menu. An action in the bar is above every action in the overflow, and the overflow is ordered the same way. Every action counts as in the bar until an operation demotes or collapses it.

With **Save**, **Cancel**, **Archive** and **Delete** in that order, a user can move Delete before Archive, pin it to the end or collapse it into the overflow menu. They can't move it right after Save, pin it to the start, or collapse Save into the overflow menu. When a user hides Cancel and then Archive, the second hide is skipped. If your page already puts Delete right after Save, operations can keep it there, but never move it above Save.

A group has at most one `primary` action. `resolveLayers` and `validateOverlay` throw a `TypeError` when two primary actions share a group.

### Telling Pliant the page's structure

To know which nodes hold a flagged one, pass the page's node tree as `baseParents`: each node id mapped to the id of the node that holds it, or `null` at the top of the page. To give the order each group renders in, pass `baseOrder`: each node id mapped to its children's ids, and each `<node>/<collection>` mapped to its item ids, in order.

```ts
const baseParents = {
  page: null,
  "checkout-actions": "page",
  save: "checkout-actions",
  cancel: "checkout-actions",
  delete: "checkout-actions",
}

const baseOrder = {
  "checkout-actions": ["save", "cancel", "delete"],
  "orders-toolbar/actions": ["export", "print", "delete"],
}
```

- A node the tree leaves out takes the parent its flag's address shows. The tree must agree with those addresses and have no cycle.
- Without `baseParents`, parents come from the addresses in your flags and in the overlays, so give each flag by its full address, such as `page/slots/checkout-actions/slots/pay-now`.
- `baseOrder` lists each child once, in the parent the tree gives it. `resolveLayers` and `validateOverlay` throw a `TypeError` otherwise.
- When `baseOrder` leaves out a group, or some of its members, an unlisted primary action goes first, an unlisted destructive one last, and other known members between them by id. If the two flagged actions are the only members Pliant knows, they start out adjacent, so users can't move the destructive action above the primary one but can move it right after it. List the group's order for the full rule.

## Shortcuts don't take reserved keys

A `bindShortcut` that takes keys the browser, the operating system or a screen reader relies on is skipped, and so is one that takes keys another action on the page already has. Keys are compared on Windows and Linux, where `mod` is Ctrl, and on macOS, where `mod` is Command, so `mod+k` and `ctrl+k` take the same keys. When two layers bind the same keys, the lower layer's binding wins.

Reserved on every page:

- Keys with no modifier, or with Shift alone.
- Alt or Alt+Shift alone, Ctrl+Alt (AltGr on Windows), and `meta` on Windows and Linux.
- `mod` with `a c d f g h j l n p q r t u v w x y z`, a digit, `=`, `-`, `[`, `]`, `tab`, `space`, `home`, `end`, `backspace`, `delete` or an arrow, and `mod+shift` with `b g n o r t w z = tab home end` or an arrow.
- `ctrl+tab` and `ctrl+shift+tab`.
- On Windows and Linux, `ctrl` with `pageup`, `pagedown` or `f4`, `mod+escape`, and `mod+shift` with `c`, `i`, `j`, `delete` or `escape`.
- On macOS, `mod` with `m`, `,`, `` ` `` or `f5`, `ctrl` with `space` or an arrow, `mod+shift` with `3`, `4`, `5`, `q`, `[` or `]`, and `mod+alt` with `c`, `d`, `i`, `j` or `escape`.

That leaves combinations such as `mod+k`, `mod+shift+k` and `mod+s`. A binding on a hidden or removed action still takes its keys. Pass the keys your app binds itself as `reservedShortcuts`:

```ts
resolveLayers(overlays, {
  surface: "/orders",
  reservedShortcuts: ["mod+k", "mod+shift+p"],
})
```

`validateOverlay` rejects a binding to reserved keys, or to your `reservedShortcuts`, even without `layers`. It catches a binding that takes another action's keys only when you pass `layers`.

## Variants keep their meaning

A `setProp` that sets a value the component's [manifest](/docs/extending/manifest) marks `semantic` is skipped, in every layer. A node flagged `destructive` also keeps its variant: any `setProp` on one of its props that has semantic values is skipped. Its other props, such as `size`, can still change.

Pass the manifests of the page's nodes by address as `manifests`. A node with no manifest isn't checked.

```ts
resolveLayers(overlays, {
  surface: "/settings",
  manifests: { "account-actions/slots/delete-account": buttonManifest },
  flags: { "account-actions/slots/delete-account": { destructive: true } },
})
```

## Form fields keep valid defaults and stay in their form

A `setDefault` on a form field must pass the field's schema. Pass each field's schema by address as `fields`: any [Standard Schema](https://standardschema.dev) that validates synchronously, such as a Zod schema. A rejected default is skipped, and a lower layer's default stays. A field with no schema isn't checked.

A `wrap` that takes a field of the form it targets is skipped, because a new parent would remount the field and lose its focus and value. A field can't `move` into another node at all, and `collapse` keeps fields where they are.
