Team and org defaults let one person arrange a page for everyone: a finance lead sets the orders table to show overdue orders first, and the whole finance team starts from that. Each member can still make their own changes on top.

## How defaults reach users

The [storage route](/docs/storage/self-hosted) returns, for each user:

- their org's overlays, from the `org` layer;
- the overlays of each team they're a member of, from the `team` layer;
- their own personal overlays.

The page stacks them, lowest first: org, then team, then personal. A user's own change wins over their team's on the same property, and the team's wins over the org's. See [Overlays and layers](/docs/concepts/layers).

`getUser` in the storage route says which org a user belongs to and which teams they're on, and `canPublish(user, target)` decides who may save a default:

</include>

`target` is `{ layer: "team", org, team }` or `{ layer: "org", org }`. `canPublish` is only asked about the user's own org and teams they belong to. Without it, nobody can publish.

## Publishing from the page

A user who may publish sees **Publish for my team** in the ask panel and the table toolbar. It turns their current arrangement into the team's default, after a preview of what teammates will see. Publishing again replaces the previous default, and **Remove team default** takes it away.

## When a user is on several teams

Pass `teamOrder` to `PliantRoot`, highest precedence first. When two teams' defaults disagree, the team listed first wins. Teams you don't list rank below the ones you do.

## Defaults aren't locks

A default is a starting point. Members can change anything it sets, for themselves. To stop changes, see [Locks for admins](/docs/guides/locks).
